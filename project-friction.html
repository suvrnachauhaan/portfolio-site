01 2024 UX · Research

Friction, Removed

Redesigned a core user flow. Cut task time by 25–30%. The best UX gets out of the way.

Overview

A B2B SaaS platform had a critical onboarding flow that was losing 40% of users before completion. The existing design had grown organically over two years — accumulating unnecessary steps, confusing copy, and redundant form fields. I was brought in to audit the entire flow and redesign it from scratch.

The goal was simple: reduce friction without reducing functionality. Every field, every screen, every micro-interaction had to earn its place.

Project Screenshot — Before & After Overview
The Problem

Users were dropping off at Step 3 of a 7-step onboarding. Heatmaps showed confusion around a multi-select input that required domain-specific knowledge most new users didn't have. Session recordings revealed users re-reading the same tooltip 3–4 times before proceeding — or abandoning entirely.

The existing team assumed the problem was copy. It wasn't. It was architecture.

Process

Research: Conducted 12 user interviews, analyzed 200+ session recordings, and mapped the existing flow against competitor onboarding patterns. Identified 3 key friction points.

Ideation: Ran a workshop with the product and engineering teams. We explored progressive disclosure, smart defaults, and conditional logic to reduce perceived complexity.

Prototyping: Built 3 variants in Figma, tested each with 8 users. Variant B — which used smart defaults and deferred non-essential fields — consistently outperformed.

Implementation: Worked closely with the front-end team to ensure transitions, validation states, and error handling matched the design spec pixel-for-pixel.

Reflection

The biggest lesson: friction isn't always visible on the surface. Sometimes it's structural — baked into the information architecture itself. The fix wasn't a better button or clearer copy. It was removing an entire step that shouldn't have existed.
